# Knowledge and Evidence Map

The architecture contains several knowledge traditions. They must not be collapsed into one undifferentiated category called “health.”

## Domain 1: Nursing and biomedical/clinical knowledge

Purpose:
- clinical and physiological grounding
- recognition of disease processes and risk
- safe nursing practice
- understanding when conventional assessment/treatment is needed
- communication with other health professionals
- referral and scope judgment

Professional status:
- directly connected to the Nursing qualification and professional practice.

## Domain 2: Nutrition science

Purpose:
- understand nutrients, metabolism, food patterns, dietary relationships, and nutrition-related health problems
- determine where nutrition can meaningfully contribute to prevention, management, recovery, or wellbeing
- determine whether deeper professional training is justified

Professional status:
- may remain supporting knowledge or become a deeper professional domain.
- deeper practice requires investigation of the relevant qualification and scope.

## Domain 3: Tibb Nabawi

Purpose:
- genuine Islamic learning
- study of material specifically attributed to the Prophet ﷺ
- understand how later scholars transmitted, interpreted, and discussed that material
- preserve the distinction between Prophetic attribution and later medical interpretation

Professional status:
- Islamic knowledge in its own right.
- health application must be considered separately from the question of religious authenticity.

The architecture does not require Tibb Nabawi to become a professional specialization.

## Domain 4: Broader natural/traditional/complementary health knowledge

Purpose:
- understand natural and traditional approaches
- investigate whole-person models of care
- study potential benefits, limitations, risks, interactions, and appropriate use
- understand how traditional knowledge may or may not relate to modern health evidence

Professional status:
- undecided.
- formal naturopathic or other complementary training is a possible future branch, not a current commitment.

## Domain 5: Evidence and research

Purpose:
- test claims
- compare sources
- understand uncertainty
- distinguish association from causation
- assess intervention evidence
- examine dose, preparation, interactions, contraindications, and adverse effects
- decide what can responsibly be communicated or recommended

This is the bridge across the other domains.

## The evidence map for future work

For any health claim, keep separate questions.

### Question A: What is the source?

Examples:
- Qur'an
- authentic Sunnah
- later scholarly commentary
- classical medical writing
- traditional practice
- modern scientific research
- expert guidance

### Question B: What does the source actually claim?

Do not upgrade a source's wording.

### Question C: What does modern evidence show?

Possible conclusions include:
- supported
- promising but uncertain
- mixed
- insufficient evidence
- evidence against
- safety concern

### Question D: What can responsibly be done with that information?

This depends on:
- benefit
- risk
- evidence quality
- patient context
- professional scope
- available alternatives
- need for referral

This four-question structure prevents two opposite errors:

1. dismissing traditional or Islamic knowledge because it is not modern research;
2. treating a traditional or religiously attributed practice as clinically proven merely because it is traditional or religiously attributed.

## Personal learning versus professional application

A subject does not need to become professionally useful in order to deserve study.

Tibb Nabawi can remain valuable Islamic learning even if a particular topic never becomes part of professional practice.

Likewise, knowledge can be retained as personal/supporting knowledge without being converted into a service.

The architecture therefore permits three outcomes:

- personal Islamic learning
- supporting professional knowledge
- deeper professional specialization

None is automatically superior.
