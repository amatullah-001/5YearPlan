# Resource Dependencies

Resources are inputs that make the architecture possible. They are not destinations, identities, or projects.

## What belongs here

Track only resources that materially affect a dependency:

- time and available capacity
- money for required education, books, software, equipment, or professional requirements
- reliable internet and computing access where remote work or digital practice requires them
- books, courses, databases, guidelines, and other learning sources
- access to appropriate mentors, supervisors, collaborators, or professional communities
- access to real users, cases, projects, or research opportunities when these are needed to develop or demonstrate competence
- tools or software that are genuinely necessary for a chosen project or capability

## Resource rule

Do not forecast resources in detail five years ahead when the dependency does not require it.

A resource belongs in the architecture when its absence could materially block a required capability, qualification, evidence milestone, or life-compatible work structure.

## Time is a resource

Time deserves special attention because the architecture must coexist with:

- Nursing education
- worship
- health and rest
- family and household responsibilities
- relationships
- future marriage and motherhood
- ordinary life

The question is not simply “How much can I fit into a year?” but:

**What amount of sustained capacity is actually available without damaging the life this work is meant to belong inside?**

## Resource versus tool

A tool is something used to perform a task. A resource is an enabling condition or input.

Neither is the destination.

If a resource becomes expensive, burdensome, or difficult to maintain, the dependency should be reconsidered rather than automatically preserving the resource.

## Review

At major gates, ask:

1. Which resource is currently limiting progress?
2. Is the limitation real or self-created by unnecessary commitments?
3. Can an existing resource be reused instead of acquiring another?
4. Does acquiring this resource unlock a genuine dependency?
5. What ongoing maintenance or cost does it create?
